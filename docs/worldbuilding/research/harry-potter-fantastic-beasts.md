# Harry Potter + Fantastic Beasts (The Wizarding World) — Worldbuilding Research

> Arcanea worldbuilding knowledgebase · researched 2026-08-08 · sources cited inline.
> Focus: extract *architecture* (mechanisms, naming systems, taxonomies) — never bricks (names, creatures, plots).

## The World in One Page

The Wizarding World's premise is the **hidden-world-within-ours**: a complete magical civilization — government (Ministry of Magic), economy (Gringotts), school (Hogwarts), press, sport, prison — running parallel to mundane Britain, concealed by the Statute of Secrecy. The genius of the premise is the **threshold fantasy**: any reader could, in principle, receive the letter. Rowling described the appeal as "a child who escapes from the confines of the adult world and goes somewhere where he has power, both literally and metaphorically" ([Sketches by Boze](https://thetalkingllama.wordpress.com/2015/08/20/what-made-the-harry-potter-books-so-successful/)).

Over **600 million copies sold** ([WordsRated](https://wordsrated.com/harry-potter-stats/)) make it the best-selling series in history and the single largest onboarding funnel into fantasy reading ever built. Librarians observed kids "reading up" beyond their age group and moving on to the wider genre ([JSTOR Daily](https://daily.jstor.org/the-enduring-popularity-of-harry-potter/)). Why it worked: (1) the hidden world requires **zero secondary-world tax** — no invented history to learn before page one; (2) mundane anchors (homework, exams, house points, train platforms) make magic legible; (3) prose clarity lets nine-year-olds follow it while Austen/Dickens/Aeschylus structures reward adults. **Arcanea note:** Arcanea is a full secondary world; the lesson is not "hide the world inside ours" but *"give every reader a mundane handle on every arcane thing"* — Gates map to lived experience (survival, creativity, love), which is Arcanea's equivalent of the Hogwarts letter.

## The Magic System

HP magic is **soft-rules magic that feels learnable**. There is no unified metaphysics — Sanderson would call it soft — but it is *packaged as curriculum*: Charms, Transfiguration, Potions, Defence Against the Dark Arts. School subjects give unruled magic the *texture* of rules: syllabi, exams (O.W.L.s/N.E.W.T.s), graded difficulty, teachers who mark you down. The reader experiences progression without the author ever committing to physics.

**The one genuinely hard subsystem is wand lore.** Wands have wood + core (phoenix feather, dragon heartstring, unicorn hair — each with defined temperament: unicorn = faithful, resists Dark magic; heartstring = powerful, temperamental; phoenix = rarest, most independent) and **allegiance rules**: "the wand chooses the wizard," and a wand won by force or disarmament transfers allegiance ([Harry Potter Wiki — Wandlore](https://harrypotter.fandom.com/wiki/Wandlore)). Because this subsystem *is* hard, Rowling could hang the entire Deathly Hallows climax on it — Draco disarmed Dumbledore, Harry disarmed Draco, therefore the Elder Wand fails Voldemort. **Mechanism: soften everything except the subsystem that must carry plot weight.**

Other limit sources: skill/talent (nonverbal magic, Patronus as advanced), **law** (Unforgivable Curses = life in Azkaban; Ministry restrictions on underage magic), and rare metaphysical limits — **Gamp's Law of Elemental Transfiguration**: food cannot be created from nothing, only summoned, transformed, or multiplied ([HP Lexicon](https://www.hp-lexicon.org/thing/gamps-law-of-elemental-transfiguration/)). Gamp's Law exists to keep scarcity (and thus stakes like the Deathly Hallows camping hunger) alive. Potions add a **craft register**: slow, ingredient-bound, temperament-bound magic — magic as artisanship rather than willpower.

**Arcanea note:** Arcanea's Gate system is already harder-ruled than HP (ten Gates, fixed frequencies, rank thresholds). Steal the *curriculum packaging* — Academy subjects, graded practice, named laws with one canonical exception each — and designate ONE subsystem (e.g., Godbeast bonding, the wand-lore analog) as fully hard so climaxes can turn on rules the reader could have deduced.

## Spell Naming Morphology

The deepest craft lesson in the franchise. Rowling (a Classics-adjacent Exeter French graduate) built incantations from **Latin and pseudo-Latin roots the reader half-knows**:

- **Expelliarmus** = *expello* ("drive out") + *arma* ("weapon"), with a "-us" ending "to make the spell sound more Latin to modern ears" ([Wizarding World](https://www.harrypotter.com/features/the-etymology-of-harry-potter-spells)) — the incantation *is* its own effect-description.
- **Lumos** from *lumen/lux* ("light"); notably she chose the invented "Lumos" over real Latin *lux*, pairing it with **Nox** — euphony beats accuracy ([Language Realm](https://www.languagerealm.com/hplang/latin_for_spells.php)).
- **Crucio** = literal Latin first-person, "I torture" — the darkest spells use *real*, unsoftened Latin; the speaker confesses the act in the casting.
- **Wingardium Leviosa**: English "wing" + Latin *arduus* ("steep/high") + *levo* ("lift") — trilingual blending for a children's-classroom charm.

**Why fake-Latin reads as learnable magic:** (1) Latin is the West's prestige register of institutional knowledge — law, medicine, liturgy — so Latinate incantations signal *magic is a discipline with a literature*; (2) the roots are half-transparent through English derivatives (expel, luminous, levitate, crucify), so every incantation is a **micro-etymology-lesson the reader solves subconsciously**, producing the "I could learn this" feeling; (3) morphological consistency (verb root + object + Latinate suffix) implies a grammar even though none is ever stated.

**Name taxonomy encodes ethics and severity:** *charms* (add/alter properties — Cheering Charm), *jinxes* (minor annoyance), *hexes* (moderate dark), *curses* (worst class — the Unforgivables). The taxonomy is fuzzy in canon but *feels* precise because the class-words themselves carry English connotation gradients. **Arcanea note:** Arcanea should define its spell classes by **which Gate powers them** (a Voice-Gate working vs. a Fire-Gate working) and build incantation morphology from a *consistent* substrate — e.g., a constructed Arcanean root-lexicon with frequency-terms — applying HP's principle: roots half-legible to English speakers, severity encoded in register (soft blends for blessings, blunt unsoftened roots for the forbidden).

## Character & Place Naming

Rowling names in the **Dickensian telling-name tradition** (Gradgrind, Pumblechook): the name is a moral dossier.

- **Malfoy** = French *mal foi*, "bad faith"; son **Draco** = Latin "dragon/serpent" ([Wizarding World — character etymology](https://www.harrypotter.com/features/etymology-behind-harry-potter-character-names)).
- **Voldemort** = French *vol de mort*, "flight/theft of death" — the character's psychology (flight FROM death) encoded in the alias he built for himself, doubled by the anagram Tom Marvolo Riddle → "I am Lord Voldemort" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voldemort)).
- **Lupin** (*lupus*, wolf — a werewolf named Wolf J. Wolf), **Sirius** (the Dog Star, for the dog Animagus), **Umbridge** (homophone of "umbrage" + *umbra*, shadow), **Dumbledore** (Old English for "bumblebee" — chosen for whimsy, not menace).
- **Alliterative founders and staff**: Godric Gryffindor, Helga Hufflepuff, Rowena Ravenclaw, Salazar Slytherin; Minerva McGonagall, Severus Snape, Filius Flitwick — alliteration marks *institutional* figures, making a large cast memorizable.

**The register-mixing technique** is the extractable mechanism: the naming palette deliberately runs from **whimsy** (Diagon Alley = "diagonally," Knockturn Alley = "nocturnally," Grimmauld Place = "grim old place" — puns that reward re-reading) to **mythic** (Voldemort, Bellatrix, Azkaban). The whimsy register keeps the world warm and British and childlike; the mythic register keeps death real. Rowling modulates between them *by subject*: shops and streets get puns; prisons, dark wizards, and prophecies get Latin/French gravity. The tonal whiplash of the series — cozy to terrifying — is executed at the level of phonemes.

**Arcanea note:** Arcanea's register is uniformly elevated (Lyssandria, Shinkami, Malachar). The HP lesson: **build a deliberate low register** — market-stall names, student slang for Gate techniques, punning tavern signs in Academy towns — so the mythic register has contrast to land against. A world with only high notes has no melody.

## Beast Taxonomy

*Fantastic Beasts and Where to Find Them* (2001) is a fictional **Hogwarts textbook** by "Newt Scamander" — an A-to-Z field guide whose engine is the **Ministry of Magic (M.O.M.) danger classification** ([Harry Potter Wiki](https://harrypotter.fandom.com/wiki/Ministry_of_Magic_Classification)):

| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| XXXXX | Known wizard killer / impossible to train or domesticate |
| XXXX | Dangerous / requires specialist knowledge |
| XXX | Competent wizard should cope |
| XX | Harmless / may be domesticated |
| X | Boring |

Three mechanisms make this sing. **(1) The scale has personality**: rating the bottom tier "Boring" turns bureaucratic taxonomy into comedy and characterizes the Ministry in one word. **(2) Footnoted dissent**: entries carry classification disputes (the unicorn is placid but classified up; the Nundu, whose breath "wiped out an entire village," anchors the top). Scamander's own footnotes argue with the Ministry ratings — the taxonomy is *contested in-world*, which makes it feel administered rather than authored. **(3) Naturalist narrator voice**: Newt writes with field-observation affection ("beast" vs. "being" definitional wrangling opens the book), habitat and temperament fields per entry, and a conservationist ethic — his proudest achievement is the Ban on Experimental Breeding ([Shmoop](https://www.shmoop.com/study-guides/literature/fantastic-beasts-and-where-to-find-them/analysis/mom-classifications)). The *Fantastic Beasts* films then weaponize the guide's thesis: most "dangerous" beasts are misunderstood; the true monster (Obscurus) is *made by repression* — taxonomy as theme.

**Arcanea note:** direct blueprint for the Arcanea bestiary + Monster System. Give Arcanea's tiers an in-world certifying body (an Academy chair? the Wardens of a Gate?), a personality-bearing scale keyed to **which Gate rank can safely face the creature** (an elegant fusion HP lacks — danger tier = required rank), *and a named in-world naturalist whose entries dispute the official ratings*. Corrupted-Void creatures (Shadow-touched) should occupy a separate axis — corruption level ≠ danger level — echoing the Obscurus insight that the worst monsters are wounded, not wicked.

## School-as-World Architecture

Hogwarts is the franchise's true protagonist-engine, providing four structures at once:

1. **Houses = instant identity + rivalry + point economy.** Sorting gives every student (and reader) a tribe on day one; the House Cup converts daily behavior into a legible score; Quidditch gives rivalry a ritual outlet. The point economy lets *any* scene carry micro-stakes ("ten points from Gryffindor").
2. **Year-structure = story-structure.** One book per school year gives free act breaks (term start, Halloween, Christmas, exams, finale), automatic escalation (age up, curriculum up, stakes up), and a renewable mystery slot.
3. **The castle as explorable mystery**: moving staircases, the Room of Requirement, the Chamber, the Marauder's Map — the school itself is a dungeon that pays out secrets at the rate the plot needs.

**Where it broke:** sorting eleven-year-olds by innate temperament is **essentialism**, and the fandom named it — "House essentialism" ([MuggleNet](https://mugglenet.com/2014/06/the-problem-with-hogwartss-house-system/)). Slytherin becomes a villain-hatchery (every named Death Eater track); Dumbledore himself muses they "sort too soon." Research on fans found Gryffindor-favoring bias baked into the text ([BPS Research Digest](https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/what-your-favourite-harry-potter-house-says-about-your-personality)). The fandom's repair — sorting reflects *what you value*, not what you are — points the way.

**Arcanea note — what the Seven Houses must do differently:** (1) **Choose, don't sort** — Houses are elected philosophies of creation, changeable between years; (2) **no villain House** — Nero House is the fertile unknown, *not* proto-Shadow (canon already insists Nero is not evil — enforce it structurally by giving Nero House heroes); (3) progression identity comes from **Gates opened** (personal, universal) while House supplies *method and community* — two orthogonal axes, so identity is never a single label; (4) Synthesis House exists precisely to break tribalism — HP has no cross-House institution until Dumbledore's Army improvises one; build it in.

## Villain Craft

**Voldemort** — root: *fear of death* (the name says so). Horcruxes literalize the theme: he mutilates his soul to avoid dying, becoming less human with each hedge. Strong mythic villain, but distant — a "fantastical" evil readers respect rather than viscerally hate.

**Umbridge** — the petty institutional villain readers hate *more* than the dark lord. The mechanism, per craft analyses ([Story Fortress](https://storyfortress.com/dolores-umbridge-how-to-write-a-villain-your-readers-will-hate/), [Wizarding World](https://www.harrypotter.com/features/why-professor-umbridge-s-sickly-villainy-terrifies-me-more-than-voldemort)): she is **(a) plausible** — every reader has met her; **(b) proximate** — she rules the daily spaces (classroom, detention) where readers live; **(c) procedurally armored** — cruelty via Educational Decrees, paperwork, and legitimacy, so resisting her feels futile in a way dueling never does; **(d) aesthetically dissonant** — kittens, pink cardigans, sweet voice wrapped around a torture quill. Voldemort attacks the body; Umbridge gaslights the truth ("you know you deserve to be punished"). Readers hate most the evil they have actually experienced.

**Grindelwald** — the seductive utopian: "For the Greater Good," a slogan he co-authored *with young Dumbledore* ([HP Lexicon](https://www.hp-lexicon.org/thing/the-greater-good/)). His menace is that he recruits by vision, not fear — he shows followers a beautiful future and accepts atrocity as its price. **Comparison to Arcanea's Malachar/Unmarred:** Malachar is already the tragic-fallen-champion (Lumina's own Luminor, broken by forced fusion with Shinkami). That covers the Voldemort/Grindelwald mythic tier. What Arcanea *lacks* is the Umbridge tier: a petty antagonist **inside the Academy** — a Decree-issuing administrator, a House-purity examiner — whose evil is procedural, survivable, and infuriating. And the Grindelwald pattern warns: the most dangerous Unmarred rhetoric should sound almost identical to Arcanea's own idealism ("imagine a good future") with one term corrupted — coercion swapped in for invitation.

## Canon Management & Fandom

The Wizarding World is the cautionary tale in **canon-tier discipline**. The seven books formed a closed, trusted canon. Then came Pottermore essays, interviews, and tweets issuing **retroactive canon**: Dumbledore's sexuality announced post-publication (2007), wizard plumbing history (2019), Nagini's origin, and more — each met with escalating fan pushback over *canonicity itself*: fans distinguish what is "authoritative or 'canon'" from authorial assertion ([Communication Research & Practice analysis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/22041451.2023.2206327); [Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/harry-potter-jk-rowling-retcon-canon-books-movies/)). The lesson: **retroactive additions that never pay costs on the page are experienced as edits to the reader's memory, not gifts.** *Cursed Child* deepened the split — "canon" by decree, rejected by much of fandom.

Meanwhile the fandom built its own infrastructure: the **Harry Potter Wiki** (harrypotter.fandom.com) maintains explicit canon-tier policy (books > author statements > films > games), per-entity infoboxes, and appearance/mention sourcing per article — a de facto canon court the IP owner never provided. **Arcanea note:** Arcanea already has the right instinct — `CANON_LOCKED.md` as single source of truth. Formalize the tiers *publicly*: Tier 1 locked canon, Tier 2 Library texts (in-world documents may contradict each other *by design* — in-world unreliability absorbs retcon pressure), Tier 3 community/expandable. Never add Tier-1 facts outside Tier-1 channels. The 17-collection Library is Arcanea's structural advantage: HP had no in-world textual tradition to metabolize disputes; Arcanea can canonize *disagreement between texts* as a feature.

## What Arcanea Should Steal (Architecture, Never Bricks)

1. **Incantation-as-etymology-lesson** — build spell names from a consistent root-lexicon (Arcanean roots + frequency terms) that English speakers half-decode; the decoding *is* the enchantment.
2. **Severity-by-register** — soft euphonic blends for blessings/charms; blunt, unsoftened roots for forbidden workings (the *Crucio* principle: the caster confesses the act).
3. **One hard subsystem** — keep most magic soft, but make Godbeast/companion bonding rules fully hard so climaxes can turn on them (the wand-allegiance play).
4. **Named laws with canonical exceptions** — an Arcanean Gamp's Law (e.g., "Anima cannot be conjured, only kindled") preserves scarcity and stakes.
5. **Curriculum packaging** — Academy subjects, graded techniques, exams; progression texture without metaphysical commitment.
6. **M.O.M.-style danger tiers keyed to rank** — Monster System tiers = minimum Gate-rank to engage; scale-labels with personality ("Boring" energy).
7. **Contested taxonomy** — an in-world certifying body + a dissenting naturalist narrator whose bestiary footnotes argue with official ratings.
8. **Field-guide narrator voice** — the bestiary written by a named in-world author with affection, habitat/temperament fields, and a conservation ethic (corruption ≠ danger as separate axes).
9. **Houses as chosen values, not sorted essence** — electable, re-electable, no villain House; identity = Gates (personal) × House (method), two axes.
10. **Cross-House institution by design** — Synthesis House as the structural anti-tribalism organ HP never built.
11. **Point-economy micro-stakes** — a legible daily score (House standing, Gate trials) that lets small scenes carry tension.
12. **The Umbridge slot** — a petty procedural villain inside the Academy; readers need an evil they've personally met.
13. **Corrupted-idealist rhetoric** — Unmarred recruitment speech should be Arcanea's own tagline with one word rotted ("build a good future" → "*impose* a good future").
14. **Register-mixing in names** — add a low/whimsy naming register (shops, slang, tavern puns) so the mythic register has contrast.
15. **Canon-tier discipline published** — locked canon / in-world texts (contradictions allowed) / community tier; new Tier-1 facts only through Tier-1 works, never announcements.

## IP Red Lines

Warner Bros. holds one of the **most aggressively enforced IP portfolios in entertainment** and has intensified cleanup ahead of the 2026 HBO series: cease-and-desist letters killed the 50,000-attendee Chestnut Hill festival, fan festivals in Illinois and New York, and even library programming ([Cinemablend](https://www.cinemablend.com/news/2437580/warner-bros-is-cracking-down-on-harry-potter-fan-festivals); [Techdirt](https://www.techdirt.com/2024/10/09/warner-bros-still-cutting-off-harry-potters-nose-to-spite-his-face/)). Precedent runs from the 2000 domain-name "PotterWar" through *WB & Rowling v. RDR Books* (blocking a print fan encyclopedia). Enforcement targets: trademarked names, places, creatures, imagery, and anything implying affiliation — not abstract structures.

**What is safe:** mechanisms, structures, and genre furniture. School-of-magic, house/faction systems, Latin-derived incantations, danger-tier bestiaries, and chosen-one arcs are unprotectable *ideas* (and pre-HP: Le Guin's Roke, *The Worst Witch*'s school). **What is not:** any protected *expression* — names (Hogwarts, Muggle, Quidditch, house names), specific creatures original to Rowling (e.g., Niffler, Horcrux), crests/iconography, or marketing that invites comparison ("Hogwarts for creators" is a trademark-adjacency risk even where copyright is clear).

**The Houses adjacency, specifically:** Arcanea's Seven Houses stay safe because they differ in *number* (7 vs 4), *basis* (elements + cosmic duality vs founder temperaments), *mechanic* (chosen philosophies + orthogonal Gate progression vs innate sorting), and *naming substrate* (Latin elemental + original cosmology vs founder surnames). Keep it that way: never four houses, never animal+color heraldry mapping (lion/red, snake/green), never a sorting artifact that reads minds, never marketing copy that names the comparison. Differentiation is strongest where Arcanea fixes HP's flaws — which conveniently is also where it is most legally distinct.
