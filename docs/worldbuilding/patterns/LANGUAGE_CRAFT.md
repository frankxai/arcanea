## 0. IP red lines (read before writing a single example)

Prose is the most copyrightable layer of a world. Systems can be studied; sentences cannot be borrowed.

- **Techniques are studiable. Text is not.** You may name what FromSoftware does with item descriptions, what Kirkbride does with contradictory scripture, what Tolkien does with the translation conceit. You may not reproduce, paraphrase closely, or "file the serial numbers off" any of their actual sentences.
- **Every demonstration in this file is original Arcanean prose written for this file.** No line below is quoted, adapted, or transposed from another franchise.
- **No synonym-lore.** TASTE.md already refuses "our Erdtree." Extend that to sentences: a cadence lifted whole from another world is a borrowed brick even when every noun is changed.
- **The named-source rule.** When citing a franchise for method, name it and describe the mechanism in your own words. Never let a citation carry an example.
- **Escalate before shipping** any passage whose distinctiveness comes from resembling a specific existing text rather than from Arcanea's own primitives.

## 1. Register discipline

A deep world does not have *a* voice. It has a set of them, each with a job, and a reader must be able to tell which one is speaking within two sentences — before any proper noun arrives. Voice-from-nowhere is the tell TASTE.md already flags; this section is how to fix it.

Every Arcanean text should be assignable to exactly one row.

| Register | Time-sense | Sentence rhythm | Characteristic failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Founding scripture | Cyclical, out of time | Long, parallel, breath-shaped; verbs of making | Grandeur without stake; typographic shouting |
| Folk saying | Perpetual present | Short, closed, memorizable; often two clauses in tension | Fortune-cookie; a proverb nobody could have been hurt by |
| Field report | This week | Flat declaratives, counts, times, hedges | Bad novelist in a uniform — reporters do not use metaphor |
| Scholarly annotation | Layered centuries | Interrupted, parenthetical, cross-referential | Certainty; a real scholar is fighting someone |
| Personal letter | One life | Uneven; digressive then abruptly plain | The letter that exists to inform the reader, not the recipient |
| Institutional record | Fiscal, procedural | Nominal, passive, formulaic; the form's own grammar | Decorated bureaucracy; the horror is in the *dullness* |
| Epitaph | Sealed | Compressed to the bone; one clause too few | Summarizing a life instead of naming one fact of it |
| Item description | Object-time, uncertain provenance | Fragmentary; one physical fact, one rumor, one gap | Explaining the object's function |

### 1.1 Founding scripture

Sounds like: creation understood as an act somebody performed and might have performed otherwise. Rhythm is breath-length — clauses that a reader can chant. Verbs do the work; adjectives are rationed.

> *And the first fire was not given. It was carried, hand to hand, by people who did not know whether the next hand was there.*

Failure mode: bolding mid-clause to manufacture weight (see §5.2, tic 3). Scripture that needs typography is not scripture.

### 1.2 Folk saying

Sounds like something a tired person says to a younger one. Two clauses, the second turning the first. It must be usable as a *rebuke*, or it is decoration.

> *Oil first, prayer after.* — Verithal, common

> *A rung you did not climb is a rung someone owes you.* — March, coastal

### 1.3 Field report

Sounds like a person with a route and a deadline. Times, counts, distances, one hedge. No metaphor at all — the horror or wonder must arrive through what is counted.

> *Third night at Cold Tallow. Lamp count 41, was 44 at the last register. Two shuttered lawful, one not accounted. Wick supply eleven days. The unaccounted lamp is the one at the ford. I have not been to the ford.*

### 1.4 Scholarly annotation

Sounds like someone with a rival. Interrupts itself. Cites. Concedes narrowly and grudgingly.

> *The Ossering fragment is generally dated to the second century of the Hush, on the strength of its wick-tax formula. The formula, however, was in continuous use for four hundred years, and Ostwin's dating rests entirely on it; I take the fragment to be later, and I take Ostwin to have known that.*

### 1.5 Personal letter

Sounds like it was written to one person who already knows most of it. Its power is what it declines to explain.

> *You will hear that I refused the seat. I refused the seat. Ask Pell what I said after, and if he tells you, do not believe the second half of it — he was not in the room for the second half. The coat you sent fits. It is the wrong grey but nobody here is counting.*

### 1.6 Institutional record

Sounds like a form filling itself in. The grammar belongs to the procedure, not to any person. This is where Arcanea's petty-institutional villainy lives (`../research/SYNTHESIS.md` U7d) — the cruelty must be *procedurally correct*.

> **WARRANT OF LAMPS — HARROW'S REST — ENTRY 1,104**
> Petitioner: Osk Berrow, wick-wright, unhoused.
> Claim: restoration of lamp-right, ford station.
> Finding: claim well-formed; claim untimely. Petitioner may refile at the next register.
> Next register: eleven years.

### 1.7 Epitaph

Sounds like the survivors ran out of stone. One fact, chosen. Never a summary of the life.

> *WEND CALLOW — kept the ford lamp nine winters — the tenth was not her fault*

### 1.8 Item description

Sounds like a cataloguer holding an object they do not fully understand. Structure: one physical fact, one attributed claim, one gap. The gap is the whole technique.

> **Ford-lamp, shuttered.** Tin body, glass replaced twice, the second time badly. The coast crews say a shuttered lamp is a shamed lamp; the Warrant of Lamps says it is only a lamp that has been closed. Both readings appear in the same inventory, thirty years apart, in the same hand.

## 2. The compression techniques

How to imply three centuries in one sentence. Each of these works by giving the reader a *load-bearing absence* to carry, which is why they collapse when overused: a text made entirely of gaps has no floor.

### 2.1 The presupposed reference

**Mechanism.** Name an event as though the reader already knows it; supply only its consequence.
**Why it works.** Familiarity is a stronger signal of age than description. A world that stops to explain itself is a world with only one reader.
**Rule.** Presuppose at most one event per short text, and never presuppose the event the piece is actually about.

> *Nobody has kept a night register at the ford since the Lampwreck, and nobody has needed to say why.*

### 2.2 The unexplained title

**Mechanism.** Give a person an office whose duties are never stated.
**Why it works.** An office implies an institution, a succession, and a reason the office was created — three structures, none of which cost you a sentence.
**Rule.** The title must be *grammatically ordinary* (a number, a place, a plain noun). Invented-word titles read as decoration and explain nothing.

> *The Fourth Reader of Harrow's Rest came out to meet the boat herself, which the crew took badly.*

### 2.3 The disputed detail

**Mechanism.** Put two sources in disagreement inside a single entry, and do not adjudicate.
**Why it works.** It converts a reader into a scholar — the mechanism Elder Scrolls proved at scale with its warring in-world histories. It also makes the surrounding, undisputed material feel *verified*.
**Rule.** Disputes must be about facts a real institution would care about (dates, who paid, who was present), not about metaphysics. Metaphysical disputes are cheap; ledger disputes are expensive and therefore convincing.

> *The March annals record eleven lamps lost. The Warrant records nine, and lists the two missing under 'transferred.' The transfer names no destination.*

### 2.4 The fossil word

**Mechanism.** Take a term whose original sense was literal and let the in-world centuries drift it. Then use both senses in the same corpus, unremarked.
**Why it works.** Nothing else so cheaply proves that time has passed *inside* the world rather than merely being asserted about it. Tolkien's place-names do this by accretion across peoples; Arcanea can do it by drift across ages.
**Rule.** Anchor the drift to something in canon that had a physical referent. Then log the drift, so contributors do not "correct" it back.

> *Two live fossils already in the corpus:*
> — **rung**: literally a step carved on the Ladder of Laws stone; by the Eighth Age, a proven claim; colloquially now, *to be runged* is to be publicly corrected.
> — **shuttered**: the formal disgrace of a Solvane lantern (`NAMING_REGISTRY.md` §5); on the coast, drifted to plain *retired*. An old inscription reading *he died shuttered* is therefore genuinely ambiguous, and the ambiguity is an asset, not an error.

### 2.5 The incomplete list

**Mechanism.** Enumerate, then stop short, and mark the stop.
**Why it works.** A closed list is inventory. An interrupted list implies a document, a damage, a censor, or a copyist who died — the reader picks, and their pick is better than yours.
**Rule.** The break must be *motivated in-world* (damage, prohibition, exhaustion). "And many others" is not this technique; it is laziness wearing its coat.

> *Of the nine who walked out from the ford: Berrow, who carried the oil. Callow, who carried Berrow. Vey, who counted. The fourth is written but the ink has gone. The fifth we are forbidden.*

### 2.6 The confident narrator who is provably wrong

**Mechanism.** Let a voice assert something the surrounding corpus quietly contradicts.
**Why it works.** It gives the world an outside — a place the reader can stand that no character occupies. It also makes institutional bias *demonstrable* instead of alleged.
**Rule.** The contradiction must be checkable from another shipped text, and the narrator must be wrong in a way that serves their interest. A narrator wrong at random is just an error.

> *A Gleam-court clerk, on the Dimmed:* **"They were driven out lawfully and by finding, each case heard, none in haste."** *— set against `../../../book/legends-of-arcanea/the-dimmed-and-the-returning-light.md`, which records that the Gleam-readers were "mortal, and political, and sometimes bought."* The clerk is not lying. The clerk has read only the Warrant.

## 3. Phonaesthetics in practice

`../../../.arcanea/lore/NAMING_REGISTRY.md` §2 assigns each register a sound-space, §3 the working-morphology tiers, §4 the collision rules. This section is the layer beneath: **why** those sound-spaces work, and what a writer or agent can actually run on a candidate.

Sound arrives before meaning. A reader hearing *Kadmor Vale* has already been told renunciation and stone before learning a single fact — which is why a register violation confuses the ear before the mind can object (Registry Law 1).

### 3.1 The four encoders

| Encoder | What it carries | Arcanean example (Registry §2) |
|---|---|---|
| **Consonant family** | Faction stance. Stops (k, d, t, p) read as refusal and boundary; liquids (l, r, y) as continuity and covenant; sibilants (s, ss, th) as depth, water, antiquity | Unmarred hard stops vs. Reaches liquid-and-`-ss-` centers |
| **Vowel weight** | Era and gravity. Dark back vowels (a, o, u) read old and heavy; front vowels (i, e) read recent, quick, small | Eldrian `-ar/-al/-ach` vs. Verithal short flats |
| **Syllable count** | Social distance. One or two syllables read local and workaday; three read formal, divine, or foreign | Verithal 1–2 vs. Gods at 3 |
| **Stress placement** | Register. Initial stress reads plain and Germanic; medial stress reads liturgical | *ÓRD-an* vs. *Lys-SÁN-dri-a* |

Corollary worth stating plainly: the Ten Gods are all three-syllable, liquid-onset, medial-stressed, open-vowelled. Nothing mortal in Arcanea may be all four at once. That is the real content of the "no cross-register borrowing" rule — it is a *prosodic* rule wearing an orthographic disguise.

### 3.2 Five tests to run on a candidate name

Run these in order. Each is cheap and each kills a different failure.

1. **The stress test.** Say it three times without looking. Where does the stress land? If it moves between repetitions, the name has no shape and readers will pronounce it differently from each other. Reject.
2. **The register-blind test.** Give the bare name to someone who knows only Arcanea's factions, no context. Ask which faction. If they cannot guess above chance, the sound is carrying nothing and you have coined decoration. (This is Registry §4's G-test, run from the reader's side rather than the writer's.)
3. **The neighbour test.** Read it in a sentence beside three LOCKED names of the *same* register, then beside three of a *different* register. It should sound at home in the first and audibly foreign in the second. A name that fits everywhere fits nowhere.
4. **The morphology test.** Strip the name to its root and try to build the tier ladder from Registry §3 — root, `-en`, `-ara`, `-aris`, Gate-compound. If the root cannot bear suffixes without becoming unpronounceable, it is a one-off label rather than a piece of language. *Rill → Rillen → Rillara → Rillaris → Rill-of-the-Flow* passes. A root ending in a consonant cluster usually does not.
5. **The genitive test.** Say the possessive and the plural out loud: *Berrow's*, *the Berrows*. Names that only work in isolation break the moment a sentence needs them to do grammatical work, which is the first thing prose asks of a name.

### 3.3 Proposed Registry extension (§8 candidate)

Per `../research/SYNTHESIS.md` U8, the Registry wants root-morpheme depth so cognates emerge instead of being assigned. Two additions belong here rather than in a separate file:

- **Etymology notes gain a stress line.** Registry §7 step 6 requires a one-line etymology, which today records morphemes only. Extend the required form to include stress and register: *"Rill: monosyllabic Verithal root, initial stress, flat vowel — Proof register, water-work."* Names whose etymology note cannot state a stress pattern have not been designed, only spelled.
- **Drift entries.** Fossil words (§2.4) need a home so contributors do not normalize them away. A `Drift` column — original sense, drifted sense, age of drift, the text where both appear — belongs alongside §5's earnable elements. `rung` and `shuttered` are the first two entries.

## 4. The Arcanean sentence

What follows is drawn from reading the actual corpus, not from theory. The strongest texts on disk are `../../../book/legends-of-arcanea/the-stilling-and-the-first-proof.md`, `../../../book/legends-of-arcanea/the-dimmed-and-the-returning-light.md`, and `../../../book/parables-of-creation/the-parable-of-the-unmarred.md`. The weaker ones are the numbered legends and the older parable set. Both groups are quoted below, honestly.

### 4.1 What the house voice does

- **Opens in the middle of a transmission.** The strong texts name an occasion before they name a fact: *"To be read when the tools you trusted stop working."* The reader is told what the text is *for* before being told what it says.
- **Grounds abstraction in a working object.** A lantern, a carved stone, a fever-bark, a wick supply. When the Stilling legend needs to convey six hundred years of loss, it reaches for "the mana crystals dimmed to river stones."
- **Concedes against itself.** *"Some deserved it. Many did not."* — the house voice admits the counter-case in its own mouth, in short sentences, without softening.
- **Withholds the ending on purpose.** *"what waits at the top of the Emberstair, no legend will tell you."* The refusal is stated as an editorial decision by the tradition, which makes it a fact about the world rather than a coyness of the author.
- **Closes on transmission, not on moral.** *"It is sung in Verithal on the night called Lantern's Vigil, and the last verse is always sung in darkness."* The colophon tells you who keeps this and when — which is the single most efficient world-building sentence in the corpus.

### 4.2 What it does — and shouldn't

Named honestly. These are real, they are frequent, and the corpus is stronger where they are absent.

**Tic 1 — the negation-correction reflex.** *"Not a god — the gods came after."* / *"Not thought—thought requires time. Not desire—desire requires form."* The move defines by exclusion. It is excellent once per text and mechanical by the third use, and `I_THE_FIRST_DAWN.md` stacks it.

**Tic 2 — chiastic clause-stacking.** *"Nero provided the canvas. Lumina provided the paint. / Nero held the potential. Lumina chose the form. / Nero was the question. Lumina was the asking."* Three identical frames in a row. Rhetorically satisfying, informationally empty — the second and third lines restate the first with new nouns.

**Tic 3 — bold as emphasis.** `0-the-first-dawn.md` bolds mid-sentence phrases roughly every third line: *"**lonely in his infinity**"*, *"**became real**"*, *"**making meaning where none exists**"*. Typography is doing the work the syntax should do. Bold belongs on terms of art on first use, never on a phrase the author wants read loudly.

**Tic 4 — "not just X but Y."** Nineteen-plus instances across `book/`. It is the highest-frequency LLM construction in the corpus (see §5, tell 2).

**Tic 5 — the explaining coda.** Every text ends in a `## The Teaching` that restates the story as instruction. The strong texts survive it because their Teaching *turns* — the Unmarred parable's coda tells you that you contain a Kadmor, which the story had not said. The older parables in `../../../book/parables-of-creation/THE_PARABLES.md` do not turn; they paraphrase. TASTE.md's explanation test applies directly: if the Teaching can be predicted from the story, cut it or make it disagree with the story.

**Tic 6 — voice from nowhere in the numbered legends.** `I_THE_FIRST_DAWN.md` runs on chapter headings and omniscient narration with no compiler, no occasion, no tradition. The lowercase-titled texts have all three. This is the cleanest available demonstration of `../research/SYNTHESIS.md` U10, and the fix is retrofittable: give each numbered legend a named compiler and an occasion of reading.

**Tic 7 — absolute inflation.** *infinite possibility*, *endless potential*, *infinite depth*, *eternal duality*, sometimes three to a paragraph. Absolutes do not scale; the fourth *infinite* in a text is smaller than the first.

### 4.3 Instead of X, write Y

Left column is real corpus text or its exact pattern. Right column is a rewrite in the same register.

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| *Nero was patient. Patience was the only quality possible when time had not begun.* | *Nero waited. There was nothing else that could be done with a time that had not started.* |
| *Nero provided the canvas. Lumina provided the paint. Nero held the potential. Lumina chose the form.* | *Nero held everything that could be. Lumina chose one of it, and the choosing is what we call the world.* |
| *its light came to rest on every maker and mender, every teacher and builder, every singer of true songs* | *its light came to rest on every maker who worked in good faith, which is a wider company than the courts ever admitted* |
| *persistence is not just virtue but mathematics* | *persistence is arithmetic wearing a virtue's coat* |
| *a wingspan of four hundred feet — wings of starlight, eyes of infinite depth* | *a wingspan that shadows a village at noon; the eyes are the part nobody describes twice* |
| *Do not fear incompletion. Let some things remain open.* (Teaching, predictable) | *The students who displayed it unfinished were later accused of cowardice. They did not defend themselves, which was either the same cowardice or the answer.* |
| *the Shadow whispered its terrible lie* | *the Shadow made its offer, and the offer was reasonable, which is the whole difficulty* |
| *Together, they were complete—the eternal duality from which all existence would spring.* | *Together they were enough. Nobody has yet explained why enough was not the end of it.* |

## 5. The AI-slop problem, linguistically

TASTE.md refuses slop at the level of *ideas* — chosen ones with no bill, prophecy as glue, tidy answers. This section refuses it at the level of *syntax*. These patterns are what makes generated prose identifiable within a paragraph, and they are not stylistic preferences: each one is a specific way of producing text that has the shape of thought without its content.

**Tell 1 — the tricolon reflex.** Three parallel items where the argument needed one or two. *Why it happens*: three is the local optimum for rhythm, so a next-token predictor finds it constantly, independent of whether a third example exists. *Repair*: keep the strongest item, delete the other two, and spend the recovered words on the one that survived. If all three are load-bearing, break the parallelism — make the third a different grammatical shape so it reads as evidence rather than cadence.

**Tell 2 — "not just X but Y."** *Why it happens*: it manufactures the *feeling* of a distinction while asserting only that Y is also true. *Repair*: assert Y alone. If X genuinely needed denying, deny it in its own sentence, and be specific about who believes X.

**Tell 3 — hedged abstraction.** *a kind of quiet reverence*, *something like grief*, *a sense of profound loss*. *Why it happens*: hedging is cheap insurance against being wrong, and abstraction is cheap insurance against having to know a detail. *Repair*: name the physical fact. *A sense of profound loss* → *she kept the wick trimmed for a man who had been dead four years*.

**Tell 4 — symmetrical paragraph lengths.** Every paragraph three to four sentences. *Why it happens*: generation regularizes toward the mean paragraph. *Repair*: force asymmetry. A one-sentence paragraph is the strongest emphasis in prose and generated text almost never uses it. The corpus's best moment is a bare line: *The working failed.*

**Tell 5 — the summarizing recap.** A closing sentence that restates the paragraph just written. *Why it happens*: it is the highest-probability continuation of any completed argument. *Repair*: delete it and read the paragraph again. It will be better. If it is not, the paragraph was the problem.

**Tell 6 — over-signposted transitions.** *It's worth noting that*, *Importantly*, *Let's now turn to*, *In this section we will*. *Why it happens*: the model is narrating its own structure because structure is easier to predict than content. *Repair*: transitions belong in headings, not in sentences. Cut the signpost and start with the noun.

**Tell 7 — adjective pairs.** *ancient and weathered*, *quiet and reverent*, *elegant and precise*. *Why it happens*: the second adjective is the first adjective's most probable neighbour, which is exactly why it adds nothing. *Repair*: keep one. Usually the less expected one. Then check whether a better *noun* would have made even that adjective unnecessary.

**Tell 8 — the closing uplift.** The final sentence that reaches for warmth: *and in that light, the work goes on*. *Why it happens*: endings statistically resolve, and generated text is drawn to the most resolved available ending. *Repair*: end on the concrete detail, or on a refusal, or one beat early. The Dimmed legend ends on *what a flame is for* and then hands the reader off to a colophon about how the margin ports tell it differently — resolution declined, twice.

**The acceptance test for this file**: none of the eight appear above. If a reader finds one, it is a bug and should be filed as one.

## 6. Dialogue and voice differentiation

Accents and verbal tics are the weakest way to distinguish characters, because they are surface features a reader stops noticing by chapter three. The durable method is to give characters different *epistemics*. Four axes, all of which survive translation, adaptation, and paraphrase:

1. **Certainty level.** How much evidence does this person need before speaking? A Prover hedges by profession; a Warden of the Unmarred states verdicts because his creed is built from one.
2. **Time-horizon.** Which tense is home? Some characters live in the next hour, some in the next generation, some in a year that is already over.
3. **What they notice first.** Entering a room, does the character see the exits, the accounts, the youngest person present, or the light?
4. **What they refuse to say.** The most identifying feature of a voice is its blind spot — the subject it routes around.

Worked example. Same event: a lamp at the ford has gone unaccounted for.

> **A Warden of the Unmarred.** "Someone took it. Say who." *(Certainty: total, from creed. Horizon: the harm already done. Notices: the missing thing. Refuses: any sentence in which the lamp's return would be good news.)*

> **A Prover of Verithal.** "Forty-one where there were forty-four. Two shuttered lawful — I have the register. The third I can account for four ways and I have tested none of them, so I will say only that it is not at the ford." *(Certainty: enumerated. Horizon: until the next test. Notices: the count. Refuses: to name a person.)*

Neither speaks in dialect. Neither has a catchphrase. A reader who has met both once can attribute either line on sight, which is the whole objective.

**Rule for agents**: before writing a two-hander, fill the four axes for both speakers in one line each. If any axis matches on both, the scene will read as one person talking to a mirror — and the fix is not to add a verbal tic but to change what one of them is afraid to say.

## 7. Translation and in-world multilingualism

Arcanea's Library is not a set of authored books. Under `../research/SYNTHESIS.md` U10 it is a set of **transmitted** ones — compiled, copied, translated, and argued over. That frame is only worth adopting if the prose actually behaves like translation, which means four visible mechanics.

**The compiler's hand.** Every collection needs a named compiler with an agenda and a century. The compiler is permitted to intrude: to note a gap, to prefer a source, to apologize for a passage they dislike but were obliged to include. One intrusion per text is plenty and it does more for depth than any amount of description.

> *— compiled at the Second Register, from the Ossering copy, which is the worse copy and the only one.*

**The untranslatable term.** Leave one word in the original per collection, and never gloss it more than once. The gloss must be a *definition of a feeling or a procedure*, not a synonym — a synonym proves the word was translatable and you were posturing.

> ***lussar*** *(Reaches): the warmth of being recognized by a road you have not walked before. The March has no word and does not miss it.*
> *Etymology note per Registry §7: `luss-` liquid onset with the covenant register's `-ss-` centre, `-ar` closing weight; initial stress. Reaches register, abstract-of-place.*

**The translator's note.** Use it to record a *loss*, never to add colour. A note that makes the text richer is an author cheating; a note that makes the text poorer is a translation.

> *[The March word here is a pun on the wick-tax. It cannot be carried over. The joke is why the passage is short.]*

**Relative distance, held consistently.** Tolkien's method is worth naming precisely because it is a method and not a text: assign each culture a consistent linguistic register and hold it absolutely, so the *distance between* cultures is audible to a reader who knows none of them. Arcanea's version already exists in Registry §2 — Verithal's plain Germanic shortness against the Reaches' liquid compounds *is* that distance. The remaining work is to stop treating those as name-generators and start treating them as the languages the texts were notionally rendered from. A Verithal text that reaches for a Reaches-shaped abstraction should feel like a borrowing, and should sometimes be marked as one.

**The discipline that makes this safe**: contradiction between collections becomes tradition rather than error — but only for facts the Library could plausibly disagree about (who was present, what was paid, which year). LOCKED canon is not a matter of transmission. Two collections may disagree about who carried the oil out of the ford. Neither may disagree about Nero.

## 8. The pass

Run this over any Arcanean text before it ships. It sits after TASTE.md's restraint tests, not instead of them.

**Register**
- [ ] The text is assignable to exactly one row of §1, and a reader could name it within two sentences.
- [ ] The occasion is stated: who reads this, when, and why.
- [ ] There is a colophon or compiler's hand — someone in the world keeps this text.

**Compression**
- [ ] At least one load-bearing absence (§2), and not more than three in a short text.
- [ ] No presupposed reference that the piece itself is about.
- [ ] Every gap is motivated in-world, not merely elided.

**Names**
- [ ] Every new name ran Registry §7 and the five tests in §3.2.
- [ ] Etymology notes state register *and* stress.
- [ ] No fossil word was silently normalized back to its original sense.

**The sentence**
- [ ] Read aloud, whole. Every sentence you stumbled on gets rewritten, not defended.
- [ ] Paragraph lengths are uneven. At least one paragraph is a single sentence.
- [ ] Negation-correction (*not X — Y*) appears at most once.
- [ ] Bold appears only on terms of art at first use.
- [ ] No absolute (*infinite*, *eternal*, *endless*) appears twice.

**Slop**
- [ ] Search the draft for *not just*, *it's worth noting*, *a sense of*, *a kind of*. Each hit is deleted or rewritten.
- [ ] No tricolon that is carrying only two ideas.
- [ ] No closing sentence that summarizes the text.
- [ ] The final line is a detail, a refusal, or a transmission — never an uplift.

**Voice**
- [ ] Every speaker's four axes (§6) differ from every other speaker's on at least two.
- [ ] Each speaker refuses to say something, and the refusal is visible.

**IP**
- [ ] No passage's distinctiveness comes from resembling a specific existing text.
- [ ] Every technique borrowed is a mechanism, described in Arcanea's own words, demonstrated with Arcanea's own prose.

Cut a third and run it again. It usually improves.
