## 1. The Hard/Soft Spectrum, Made Practical

Sanderson's three laws are the standard frame and they get quoted as slogans, which drains them. Stated as engineering: conflict-resolution power scales with reader *understanding* (predictable magic makes a clever solution read as earned rather than as a rescue); limitations beat powers (what a character cannot do is where the scene lives); and expand before you add (depth in existing rules produces more story per unit of complexity than new rules do).

### What each end actually buys

**A hard rule buys predictability, and predictability buys the reader's participation.** When the reader holds the rule, they run the scenario ahead of the character — and when the character finds the move the reader almost found, the reader feels smart rather than managed. This is the entire mechanism behind the Deathly Hallows climax: wand allegiance is stated years earlier, so Harry's argument about who disarmed whom lands as deduction, not as a save. Hard rules also make betrayal legible. Cheating a stated law identifies your villain without a speech.

**A soft rule buys awe, threat, and mystery — and it buys them by refusing prediction.** Gandalf is terrifying because we do not know the ceiling. Tolkien's Istari were forbidden to dominate; Gandalf casts almost nothing on-page, and the audience calibrates his power by *what he dares not do*. Soft magic cannot solve a plot problem without cheating, so it is used to create them — it raises stakes and gets out of the way.

**The working principle**, and the reason this section exists: **a system needs exactly ONE hard subsystem carrying plot weight. The rest should stay soft.** Harry Potter is the proof by construction — a system with no unified metaphysics at all, packaged as school curriculum so it *feels* ruled (syllabi, exams, graded difficulty), with wandlore as the single genuinely hard component. One hard rule was enough to carry a seven-book climax. Hardening everything is a common failure: it converts a world into a rulebook and pays awe for arithmetic.

### Where each researched world sits, and what it pays

| World | Position | What it buys | What it pays |
|---|---|---|---|
| **FMA** | Hard core, hard theme | Equivalent exchange is stated in episode one, so every villain is a legible *cheat* against it (Philosopher's Stones = souls as counterfeit payment) and the finale can transcend it honestly | Almost no mystery in the magic itself; the wonder has to come from the ethics instead |
| **JJK** | Hard mechanic, soft substrate | Sworn self-restriction is a verb characters use *tactically* — every fight becomes contract law | Rule-lawyering density; new readers meet an explainer before they meet a feeling |
| **Demon Slayer** | Hard-ish, pedagogical | A teachable technique tree generates schools, rivalries, and characterization automatically | Very low ceiling on cosmic awe — the magic is disciplined breathing, deliberately |
| **Harry Potter** | Soft, one hard subsystem | Curriculum texture without metaphysical commitment; wandlore carries the climax | Continuity fights forever; readers can always ask "why not just use X" |
| **Elder Scrolls** | Soft cosmology, hard economies | Magicka enters through a scar in the creation myth (every spell restates the cosmology); soul gems force ethics into the fuel supply | Schools of magic are institutional, argued-about, and revised — which is a feature, but means no stable taxonomy |
| **Final Fantasy** | Constant interface, world-derived implementation | "Fire" exists everywhere; where fire *comes from* is each world's thesis (dead espers, a dying planet, petrifying aether) | Nothing accretes across worlds — depth is per-entry, never cumulative |
| **Warcraft** | Chart-hard, story-soft — until it wasn't | The six-force chart is a private consistency tool that keeps thirty years of writers aligned | Shadowlands walked players *into* the chart and demoted every god to an NPC with a loot table |
| **Tolkien** | Soft, thematic law | Power is what a being *is*, not what it casts; magic used to make and bless is good, the same power used to coerce is machinery | Cannot support tactical plotting at all — no fight is ever won by the magic |
| **Avatar / Arcane** | Hard mechanic, cultural depth | Element derives an entire culture — art, philosophy, economy, movement grammar | The hardness is physical, not metaphysical; the cosmology stays thin by comparison |

The pattern across the table: **the systems people call "well-built" are not the hardest ones. They are the ones where the hard part and the theme are the same object.** Equivalent exchange *is* FMA's argument about grief. Materia *is* FF7's argument about extraction. Wandlore is the exception that proves the rest — a hard subsystem that means nothing thematically, which is why it works as a plot device and nothing more.

## 2. The Mechanism Catalogue

Reusable machinery. Each entry: the mechanism, the worlds that demonstrate it, and the design rule you can carry into a new system.

### 2.1 Cost — paid by whom, when, and whether anyone can see it

Three questions decide a cost's dramatic value. **Who pays** (the caster, a third party, the world, the future). **When** (at cast, at threshold, on a delay, never). **Is it visible** (to the caster, to bystanders, to the reader).

An invisible cost is not a cost; it is an author's private note. FF16 petrifies its casters *on their skin* — the audience watches the bill accumulate. Warcraft's fel corruption glows green. Arcanea's own Fractured carry Hollow Frequency Syndrome with "no off position."

**Rule:** every cost must have a *tell* — something a bystander in the scene could point at. If you cannot draw it, the reader cannot feel it.

### 2.2 The conservation law

State a law of exchange early, then let every antagonist be a counterfeiter against it. FMA is the canonical build: "to obtain, something of equal value must be lost" is first physics, then a moral code the brothers try to live by, then a thesis the story interrogates — and the finale's answer (a trade of talent for a brother) argues some things exceed arithmetic. Crucially, the law is *falsifiable in-world*: Philosopher's Stones appear to break it, and the horror is discovering they don't — they just move the payment onto other people.

**Rule:** a conservation law is worth ten limitation rules, because it turns your villains into an argument instead of a threat. But only state one you are willing to have the finale test.

### 2.3 The sworn-restriction trade

Power purchased by permanent, voluntary self-limitation. JJK's implementation: strictness of the restriction scales the power gained; reveal your technique's workings to an opponent and it hits harder; break your own restriction and the advantage vanishes instantly; break one made with another party and the consequences are dire. This is Sanderson's Second Law converted into an in-world verb characters can *use* — every power-up becomes a sacrifice, every duel becomes contract law.

**Rule:** the trade only carries plot weight if breaking it is *detectable*. Design the enforcement before the mechanic. (Arcanea's application and its problems: §4.)

### 2.4 The resource economy vs. the toll economy

Two different machines that both get called "cost."

A **resource economy** is a pool you spend down and refill: magicka, aether, materia charges, cursed energy. It produces attrition, pacing, and logistics — you plan, you ration, you run dry at the worst moment.

A **toll economy** is a price paid at a threshold, once, and often permanently: a soul consumed, a restriction sworn, a Gate crossed. It produces decisions — the scene is *whether to pay*, not whether you can afford it.

Elder Scrolls runs both and keeps them separate: magicka is a resource; enchanting requires a trapped soul, and sentient souls are the most potent and the most morally radioactive. The two economies do different narrative work and should never be collapsed into one number.

**Rule:** decide which economy each subsystem is, and say so. A world can hold both; a single mechanic cannot be both without becoming mush.

### 2.5 Corruption as interest rate

Cost deferred, compounding, and paid by a later version of the user. Warcraft's arcane addiction and fel corruption; FF16's petrification; Arcanea's own Kurusei Iron, which "whispers, wants to be wielded, grants power at cost of corruption." The mechanic's virtue is that it makes the *character* the ledger — the more they borrow, the less of them remains to enjoy the winnings.

The trap is moral flatness. If corruption always equals evil, the mechanic degenerates into an alignment meter. Arcanea has already dodged this at the cosmological level: Nero is not evil; only Shadow (corrupted Void) is. Keep that discipline at the mechanical level too.

**Rule:** corruption must offer something the character genuinely needed, and it must be survivable at low doses, or nobody ever borrows and the mechanic never fires.

### 2.6 The gating question — skill, bloodline, oath, bargain, or catastrophe

Every system answers "who may wield this," and the answer sets the political shape of the world.

- **Skill** (Demon Slayer's breathing forms, HP's curriculum) → schools, teachers, lineages, meritocratic politics.
- **Bloodline** (Marvel's mutants, Arcanea's Arcans by blood) → dynasties, prejudice, the persecution plot.
- **Oath** (JJK's restrictions, monastic vows) → institutions, tribunals, apostasy.
- **Bargain** (Faust, warlocks, soul gems) → patrons, debts, third parties with agendas.
- **Catastrophe** (Marvel's Terrigen Bomb, Arcanea's Gate Storm and Vael Rain) → mass empowerment, registration, refugees.

Most durable worlds run two or three at once and let them *conflict*. That conflict is the faction map.

**Rule:** name your gates explicitly. A system with an unstated gating rule will drift toward "whoever the plot needs," which readers detect as unfairness before they can articulate it.

### 2.7 Comprehension-priced power

Power equal to understanding, paid in time or in something worse. The Thu'um: shouts are words of the dragon language, and *understanding* a word — not pronouncing it — is what arms it. Mortals need decades of meditation per word; the shortcut is consuming souls that already carry the understanding. Clean cost model, and it doubles as characterization — the pacifist Greybeards can barely speak without leveling a town.

**Rule:** when power is comprehension, the villain's route is always *theft of comprehension*. That gives you a second antagonist archetype for free.

### 2.8 Magic as being, not casting

Tolkien's elves reject the word "magic"; their art is continuous with their nature. Power flows from what a being *is* — its order and spiritual stature — not from a spell list, and Letter 131 states the moral axis: Art not Power, sub-creation not domination. The position buys enormous dignity, costs all tactical utility, and produces the strongest restraint effect in fantasy — the audience calibrates power by refusals.

**Rule:** if any part of your system is being-based rather than casting-based, that part must never solve a plot problem. It exists to make the reader afraid of, or moved by, a person.

### 2.9 Externalized power

Pour your strength into an object and you gain reach and acquire a fatal dependency. Sauron's One Ring is the complete build: amplification, plus destructibility, plus the trap that it cannot be used against its maker without corrupting the wielder — the ultimate weapon is a snare for the good.

**Rule:** every externalization creates a *place to attack*. If your world has artifacts and no one has ever tried to break one, the artifacts are props.

### 2.10 Ethically loaded fuel, and belief that binds

Two mechanisms that both put the moral weight into the supply chain instead of into a speech. **Fuel that consumes persons**: soul gems, where sentient souls are the strongest and the most damning; FF6's magicite, strip-mined from dying espers and industrialized by an Empire; FF7's Lifestream, the planet's soul monetized as electricity. **Belief that manifests and then captures**: FF14's primals are what the faithful *believe* the god to be, and they "temper" their worshippers — belief becomes a self-reinforcing epistemic trap. The second is the most underused mechanism in this catalogue and the one that fits Arcanea best, which already runs belief-powered machinery in the belief-gauge on Malachar's seal.

**Rule:** if you want a magic system to argue, make the fuel a victim; if belief powers anything, decide what belief does *to the believer*. A one-directional belief mechanic is half a mechanic.

### 2.11 The escalation valve

How a system stays interesting at high power without breaking. Four working valves:

1. **Depth over addition** (Sanderson's Third Law) — new consequences of old rules, not new rules. Demon Slayer's derived styles are all recombinations of five fundamentals, and each derivation is a character study.
2. **The morphological ladder** — FF's tiered spell suffixes let players parse a spell they have never seen, so power reads as grammar rather than as escalation. Cheap, scalable, and it makes rank *audible*.
3. **Mutual exclusivity** — the ceiling is not power but coherence. If deep specialization forecloses other paths, the strongest characters become the most *constrained*, which is a story, not an arms race.
4. **Change the stakes axis** — at the top of the power ladder, stop measuring strength. Frieren's mages are functionally maxed; the tension moved to time and grief.

The failing valve is the innate spike — a character simply becomes stronger because the plot needs it. `../TASTE.md` names this on the refusal list: *"Power-scaling arms races. Escalation must be causal and paid for, never innate spikes."*

**Rule:** write down which valve you are using before Arc 3. Retrofitting a valve after a power ceiling is breached is the most expensive repair in serial fiction.

### 2.12 Legibility ladders

There are three different truths in any magic system, and confusing them is a craft error:

| Layer | Who holds it | Property |
|---|---|---|
| **What the audience is told** | The reader | Must be sufficient to predict the ONE hard subsystem, and nothing more |
| **What practitioners believe** | In-world casters and institutions | Should be *partly wrong*, and organized politically |
| **What is actually true** | The author's private chart | Never published, never walked into |

Elder Scrolls does the middle layer best: schools of magic are the Mages Guild's taxonomy, argued about in-world and revised between games with diegetic justification. Reclassification is a political act, not an author's correction. Warcraft holds the third layer as a private consistency chart — and Shadowlands is what happens when you take the audience on a tour of it.

**Rule:** publish the practitioner layer as *contested*, and treat the true layer as a horizon, not a destination.

### 2.13 The "why hasn't this solved everything" pressure test

The standing audit. For every capability in the system, ask: given this exists and has existed for centuries, why is the world not already reorganized around it? If healing works, why does anyone die of anything? If Sight works, why are there secrets? If Voice compels truth, why are there courts?

Rowling's answer is Gamp's Law of Elemental Transfiguration — food cannot be created from nothing — a named law with one canonical exception, existing purely so scarcity and therefore stakes survive.

**Rule:** every capability needs either a named law that bounds it, an institution that monopolizes it, or a cost that makes routine use irrational. Run the audit on the whole capability list, not just the flashy ones — the mundane capability is usually the one that quietly dissolves your economy.

## 3. Failure Modes

The seven ways a magic system rots, each with the world that demonstrated it.

**Power creep.** Escalation without causality. Every arc's threat larger, every solution stronger, no new cost. Detection: if you can no longer state what a mid-tier practitioner *cannot* do, creep has already happened. Repair is always expensive and usually requires a catastrophe (Marvel had to detonate its own mutant population to restore scarcity — see §3, "the spigot problem," in `../research/marvel.md`).

**The deus-ex-machina spell.** A capability introduced in the same scene where it resolves the conflict. Sanderson's First Law is the exact diagnostic: the reader must have been able to predict it. The tell is a character explaining a rule *during* the climax.

**The rule that exists only to be broken.** A law stated once, ceremonially, so the finale can violate it "meaningfully." Readers detect the setup because the rule has no other consequences in the text. A law is credible only if it inconvenienced somebody in an ordinary scene first.

**Unlimited power with a vague cost.** "It drains him." "There is always a price." The cost with no unit, no tell, and no ledger. This is the most common failure in AI-assisted worldbuilding specifically, because vague cost language is cheap to generate and reads as depth on a single pass. `../TASTE.md`: *"every power has a cost, every faction an internal fault line, every belief a price. Anything free is beige."*

**Plots that contradict their own stated rules.** The system says X; a beloved scene requires not-X; the scene ships. Every long franchise accrues these. The two survivable responses are the unreliable-narrator device (Warcraft's Chronicle reframed as the titans' point of view, not omniscient truth) and one loud, complete repair (Runeterra). Silent edits are the worst option — they teach the audience that the record is negotiable.

**Explained until it stopped being magic.** `../research/SYNTHESIS.md` §4 names this trap first, and cites the Shadowlands case: taking the audience into the afterlife machinery, revealing the architects, and shrinking every god to an NPC. The extracted law in `../research/warcraft.md` is the one to memorize — **a cosmology chart is a horizon, not a destination.** The chart exists so writers stay consistent, not so readers can tour it.

**Moralized mechanics.** A subtler failure: when a material condition is written as a moral one. If a character's power broke because of a mineral, and the text treats that as a failure of character, the system has quietly become a morality meter. Arcanea is exposed here specifically — see the Fractured, §4.4.

## 4. Arcanea Application

Everything below is STAGING and additive. The Ten Gates and their frequencies, the ranks, the Five Elements with the Void/Spirit duality, the Houses, the Godbeast bonds, and the Lumina/Nero duality are LOCKED in `../../../.arcanea/lore/CANON_LOCKED.md` and are used here exactly as written. The spell taxonomy in `../../../.arcanea/lore/MAGIC_SYSTEM.md` (LOCKED 2026-06-23) is likewise treated as fixed.

### 4.1 What Arcanea already has, mechanically

More than the 6/10 score suggests, and it is worth naming precisely so nothing gets rebuilt:

- **A gating ladder with real thresholds** — ten Gates, unique frequencies, five ranks. Legible.
- **A locked tier gate** — `MAGIC_SYSTEM.md` binds spell tier to Gates open (Light→Divine, 1–2 through 10). A caster cannot wield a working whose Gate they have not opened. That is already a hard rule.
- **A discipline triangle** — Defense abjures Attack of equal-or-lower tier; Attack disrupts Summoning mid-cast; Summoning overwhelms Defense by attrition. Tactically predictable, which is what §1 asks for.
- **A Guardian-as-witness motif, already locked** — Divine workings may name a Guardian as witness, never as caster. This is a small line with large implications, and §4.3 builds on it rather than inventing anything.
- **A materials economy** — Vael Crystals, Luminor Metals, Nero Shards, with mutation outcomes.
- **A cost vocabulary that already exists in staged canon** — Draconite "costs vitality, memory, years"; Kurusei Iron grants power at cost of corruption; Hollow Frequency Syndrome has "no off position"; the Quieting Vow is "irreversible and slowly painful."

What is missing is not cost. It is a **law** that makes those scattered costs one system.

### 4.2 The one hard subsystem: recommendation

Three candidates present themselves — the oath layer (SYNTHESIS U3), Godbeast bonding (the wandlore analog suggested in `../research/harry-potter-fantastic-beasts.md`), and the frequency ladder itself.

**Recommendation: the payment ledger on Gate-opening.** Not the vow — the *ledger*. The distinction matters and it is the central proposal of this file.

> **Every Gate that opens is paid for. The only question is whether the payer chose the currency.**

This is a conservation law (§2.2), not a limiter. It is falsifiable in-world, it makes Malachar a counterfeiter rather than merely a villain, and — critically — it accommodates the parts of Arcanea that a pure oath mechanic contradicts. Under it:

- **The negotiated payment** is a standing restriction sworn at the threshold: a named thing the practitioner will not do, for as long as the Gate stays open. Chosen, articulated, witnessed.
- **The imposed payment** is what the Gate takes when nobody negotiated: unmodulated frequency, constant bleed of the Gate's domain into the practitioner's inner life, and — at the extreme — cascade. `GATE_TOUCHED_UNDERGROUND.md` has already written this in detail without calling it a payment. A Sparked practitioner "cannot turn it down"; a Sparked Voice-Gate individual hears the truth-content of every conversation in range, always. That is a bill, arriving unasked.
- **The refused payment** is Malachar. `CANON_LOCKED.md` states he was rejected by Shinkami when attempting *forced fusion* with the Source Gate. Under the ledger, forced fusion is the attempt to open without paying — and Shinkami's rejection is not a moral verdict but a mechanical one. The Source Gate does not extend credit.

The conservation clause — where the payment *goes* — is where this connects to the frequency ladder without adding cosmology. The withheld capacity is what narrows the band. A narrow band carries further. This is an acoustic statement, not a metaphysical one, and it sits comfortably beneath U1's proposed First Duet without requiring it.

### 4.3 Answers to the four questions

**What exactly does opening a Gate cost?** A standing restriction for the life of the opening, in the practitioner's own terms if they negotiated it, in the Gate's terms if they did not. Plus a one-time threshold toll — the passage itself, which nobody escapes and which is what makes an opening an event rather than a milestone.

**Why are Luminors rare, mechanically?** Not because ten is a lot. Because **ten kept prices must be mutually satisfiable.** A Fire-Gate restriction that forbids an act and a Heart-Gate restriction that requires it cannot coexist in one life; the practitioner who opens the second one loses the first. Most sets of ten collide. The tenth Gate is therefore a problem of *coherence*, not of strength — which is why Luminor reads as the whole chord rather than the loudest note, and why the rank has always been described as an attainment rather than a power level. This also gives the Academies a real curriculum problem to argue about: which restrictions compose, and who decides.

**What stops a Master from simply doing everything?** Three brakes, two of them already locked. (1) The tier gate: 5–6 Gates caps at Greater-tier workings; Sacred and above are structurally out of reach. (2) The discipline triangle: every capability has a counter at equal tier. (3) The new one: five or six simultaneous standing restrictions. A Master's action space is *narrower* than a Mage's in specific, named ways — that is the point of the design. Power grows; permission shrinks. Every rank-up should read as a door opening and a door closing.

**Where does this leave the Guardians?** Witnessing, exactly as `MAGIC_SYSTEM.md` already permits — and off-page, per the Shadowlands law. A Guardian does not attend commencements. The witness is a *resonance signature*: the Gate's own frequency carries the record, which is why attunement-capable practitioners can read an opening the way `GATE_TOUCHED_UNDERGROUND.md` already says they can — "the way you can see a door that has been knocked off its hinges." The god never appears. The evidence does.

### 4.4 Pressure-testing SYNTHESIS U3

U3 proposes sworn self-restriction as the answer and calls it the highest-priority upgrade in the slate. Most of that is right. Some of it breaks against canon that already exists.

**Where it holds.**
- The mechanism is genuinely thematic for Arcanea. A universe about creation should price creation, and the price should be a *shape* accepted rather than a wound taken.
- It gives the Guardians a mechanical function that does not require putting them on screen — and it extends a locked motif rather than inventing one.
- It reframes the Registry debate cleanly. Under U3, Gate-Touched instability has a mechanical cause rather than a moral one, which converts a prejudice plot into an argument about who was ever offered a choice. `GATE_TOUCHED_UNDERGROUND.md` §"Why Society Fears the Gate-Touched" is already built for exactly this reframe.
- It makes the Quieting Vow legible as a peer rite rather than an oddity — the Unmarred pay the price of *closing*, in the same currency, to the same ledger.

**Where it strains.**
1. **Two gating systems that do not interact.** `MAGIC_SYSTEM.md` already gates by Gate and rank. Adding an oath layer without a composition rule produces a system with two brakes and no stated relationship. §4.3 offers one (tier gates capability, restriction gates permission) — but it must be stated or it will drift.
2. **The witness bottleneck.** "Witnessed by that Gate's Guardian" cannot mean attendance without either flooding ten gods with ceremony duty or putting them on-page constantly, which violates the Shadowlands law the same SYNTHESIS document lists as trap #1. §4.3's resonance-signature reading resolves this, but U3 as written does not.
3. **It hands the Unmarred a mechanical proof.** If power *requires* binding, then Kadmor Vale is not merely sympathetic — he is correct that the Gates manufacture bondage, and the synthesis arc in `../../../.arcanea/lore/THE_UNMARRED.md` gets much harder to land. This is recoverable and probably desirable (a stronger antagonist argument is an asset), but it is a thematic cost that U3 does not price.
4. **No enforcement surface.** JJK's version works because breaking a vow has instant, visible consequences. Arcanea has no verification technology named. A hard subsystem the reader cannot check is not hard.

**What it fails to explain.**

1. **The unoathed Luminor.** This is the strongest objection, and it is not hypothetical — it is already written. `GATE_TOUCHED_UNDERGROUND.md` states that of roughly seven confirmed Ascendants, **three became Luminors, "the only Gate-Touched individuals known to have reached the highest rank without any formal Academy training."** If ranks measure accumulated binding, those three cannot exist. U3's central claim contradicts staged canon in a document the same research swarm cites — and, more seriously, it deviates from the vault: `CANON_LOCKED.md`'s rank table is headed **"Gates Open | Rank."** Rank is *defined* as a count of openings. "Ranks measure accumulated binding" is not an addition to that definition; it is a replacement for it, which puts U3 outside the additive-only rule it claims to observe. The ledger framing in §4.2 repairs it — those three paid in full, in the imposed currency, and the existing text already describes what that costs them ("the constant demand of maintaining the integration") — but *"ranks measure accumulated binding"* has to be withdrawn and replaced with *"ranks count Gates opened; every opening is paid for."*
2. **Seven of the eight origin classes.** The Awakened, Synths, Bonded, Celestials, Voidtouched, Architects, and Gate-Touched do not walk the Academy path. Vael Crystals, Resonance Bloom, and Godbeast bonds all confer capability with no oath in sight. U3 as stated is a rule for Arcans. The ledger framing generalizes; the vow framing does not.
3. **The Fractured.** Their Gates opened *and broke* — a material event, caused by Nero Shard contamination. An oath system's vocabulary for a broken channel is oath-breaking, which is a moral category. Mapping the two would moralize an injury (§3, moralized mechanics), and would be a serious error in a universe whose Fractured are explicitly written as tragic rather than culpable. The ledger must state plainly that a fracture is a payment *interrupted*, not a promise broken.
4. **Where the payment goes.** U3 describes a limiter, not a conservation law. FMA's exchange balances; a self-restriction paid to nobody just caps output. §4.2's acoustic clause is one answer; any answer is better than none.

**Net:** adopt the mechanic, demote the rank claim, generalize the frame to a ledger, and specify the witness as a signature rather than an attendance.

### 4.5 The naming problem, and three candidates

U3 carries an explicit IP-differentiation requirement: the sworn-self-restriction mechanic's common English name is another franchise's exact localized term, and a term is a brick, not architecture. The mechanic must never ship under that name in-world.

Coined per the procedure in `../../../.arcanea/lore/NAMING_REGISTRY.md` §7. **Register: Institutions** (§2 — English compound, initial caps, no invented words), which is also the register that produced the Quieting Vow and the Grey Bench. Sibling-sound to the Quieting Vow is deliberate: these are the same kind of rite, sworn in opposite directions.

| Candidate | Etymology note (required by §7) |
|---|---|
| **the Narrowing Oath** | "Narrow" (OE *nearu*, close/constricted) + "oath"; Institutions register, [Quality]+[Noun]. The metaphor is acoustic, not ascetic — narrowing the band is what lets a tone carry to depth. |
| **the Held Word** | "Held" doubles as a sustained tone and a kept promise; "word" is the spoken restriction. Germanic-plain and monosyllabic, borrowing the March/Verithal weight the setting already uses for its most serious human institutions. |
| **the Forswearing** | From OE *forswerian*, to renounce under oath — and, in the same word, to perjure. The double sense is the point: one term names both the keeping and the breaking, and the Academies argue about whether that was deliberate. |

Collision check (§4): no candidate falls within edit-distance 2 of any Locked name; none is an invented word; none borrows a God, Godbeast, Eldrian, or Wisdom register. Superseded list (§6) clear — no adjacency to Amaterasu or Thessara. G-test sentence: *"Kadmor Vale swore the Quieting Vow; Lyria's Gate took the Narrowing Oath; Malachar offered neither."* Same universe, same register.

The placeholder terms floated in `../research/anime-modern-worlds.md` §"IP Red Lines" were rejection-avoidance examples, not coinages, and should not ship.

## 5. IP Red Lines

`../BEST_PRACTICES.md` §IV states the working rule: **copy the architecture, never the bricks** — adopt the ontology, the delivery grammar, and the dialectic, then re-derive every name, mechanism, cost, and consequence from Arcanea's own primitives. This section is stricter than usual because the sworn-restriction mechanic sits closer to a single identifiable source than anything else in the ten-upgrade slate.

**Never reuse — the term itself.** The franchise's localized two-word term for the mechanic is protected expression and functions as trade dress in practice. It must not appear in Library texts, product copy, spell corpora, the `magic-intelligence-system/` schema, agent prompts, or marketing. It may appear in this research directory and in `../research/anime-modern-worlds.md`, which is commentary, cited, and never shipped.

**Never reuse — the clause taxonomy.** The source's specific enumerated categories of restriction (the named varieties, their stated exchange rates, the specific two-party/self-party split with its distinct penalty schedules) are the expressive elaboration, not the idea. Arcanea's restrictions must be derived from the Ten Gates' own domains — a Voice-Gate restriction should be recognizably a *Voice-Gate* thing, growing out of truth and expression, not a re-skinned import.

**Never reuse — the mechanic-plus-name pairing.** This is the specific trap for U3. "Voluntary self-restriction increases power" is an unprotectable idea with a long pre-modern lineage (monastic vows, geasa, taboo-bound heroes, ascetic discipline traditions worldwide). What is protected is that idea *wearing that name*, with those clause types, adjudicated that way. Break the pairing at all three joints and the transfer is architecture. Keep any one joint intact and it is a borrowed brick with a new sticker — which `../TASTE.md` refuses on sight as synonym-lore.

**Also off-limits, from adjacent systems in this file:** "equivalent exchange" verbatim; the FF tier-suffix ladder's specific morphemes (Arcanea has its own in `NAMING_REGISTRY.md` §3); domain/curse/technique vocabulary from the same source franchise; Warcraft's six-force chart labels and layout; TES's coined terms and any word of its dragon conlang; HP's spell names and named laws (an Arcanean scarcity law must be re-derived, not renamed).

**Safe to take, all of it architecture:** conservation laws, toll vs. resource economies, corruption-as-interest, comprehension-priced power, gating taxonomies, escalation valves, legibility ladders, named-law-with-exception, the one-hard-subsystem doctrine, and the pressure-test discipline in §2.13. Solfeggio frequencies (174–1111 Hz) are public domain and already locked.

**The two-step test before shipping any mechanism in this file** (from `../research/anime-modern-worlds.md`): (1) would a fan of the source recognize the *specific expression*? (2) does the element survive renaming into pure mechanism? Ship only what passes both.
