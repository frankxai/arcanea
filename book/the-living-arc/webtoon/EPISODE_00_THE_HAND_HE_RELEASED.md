## Episode contract

Before Malachar becomes the antagonist who would end choice, he honors Saelith’s right to choose and loses her. The reader must finish the episode understanding both why releasing her hand was morally necessary and why he later comes to regard that release as cowardice.

The episode does not argue that Saelith’s death was good. It establishes that saving her through bodily override would have made her survival belong to Malachar rather than to her.

## Vertical rhythm

| Movement | Panels | Rhythm | Turn |

| The hand released | 29–38 | Extreme vertical space | He honors the choice and the city lives |

## Panel script

### Movement I — The failing gate

**01 — Full-width establishing panel**

Eiren Gate at sunset. Five elemental seams are pulling apart above the lower city. Tiny people descend the last intact road.

**Caption:** Eiren Gate failed at sunset.

**02 — Narrow vertical panel**

The lower road bends under a wave of pressure.

**SFX:** *THRUM*

**03 — Close-up**

Saelith’s boots slide backward across wet moonbone.

**04 — Medium panel**

Saelith has one hand inside the seam. Her other arm signals evacuees forward. She is wounded, focused, and making decisions.

**Caption:** The lower city was still running.

**05 — Long drop**

The road below her. Families, wardens, and wounded people moving toward the open gate.

**06 — Detail**

A child loses a carved animal. Saelith catches it with her heel without turning from the seam.

**07 — Detail**

She kicks the carving back toward the child.

**08 — Silent reaction**

The child catches it and keeps running.

**09 — Wide panel**

Malachar arrives through white-gold light above the failing platform, still Lumina’s champion.

**10 — Close-up**

He sees the blood along Saelith’s sleeve.

**11 — Close-up**

Saelith sees him see it. She does not apologize.

**12 — Black gutter with one balloon**

**Malachar:** Take my hand.

### Movement II — The impossible rescue

**13 — Medium two-shot**

Saelith shifts her free hand into his without removing the hand inside the seam.

**Saelith:** I am.

**14 — Close on Malachar**

**Malachar:** No. Take it and come with me.

**15 — Wide panel**

Another piece of the road collapses. Saelith tightens her stance.

**Saelith:** If I move, the lower road closes.

**16 — Detail**

White-gold frequency gathers around Malachar’s thumb where it touches her wrist.

**17 — Narrow panel**

Inside Saelith’s arm: nerves, bone, and muscle rendered as luminous structural lines.

**Malachar:** I can change what your body will permit.

**18 — Close on Saelith**

She is afraid. The fear does not make the answer less hers.

**Saelith:** That is not saving me.

**19 — Close on Malachar**

**Malachar:** It is keeping you alive.

**20 — Saelith reaction**

The distinction wounds her more than the pain.

**Saelith:** Alive for whom?

**21 — Full-width vision panel**

Malachar’s infinite sight opens. Hundreds of translucent futures radiate behind Saelith.

**22 — Stacked micro-panels**

In one future Saelith dies. In another, a bridge warden dies. In another, an unnamed stranger holds the seam.

**23 — Long vertical cascade**

Different faces. The same reaching hand. The same cost.

**Caption:** In every future where each person remained fully themselves, someone stayed.

**24 — Tight close-up**

Malachar’s eyes return to the present.

**Malachar:** I will not watch you die.

**25 — Saelith point of view**

The escaping city reflected in his eyes.

**Saelith:** Then look at the people who live.

**26 — Silent half-page**

They listen to footsteps crossing the lower road.

**27 — Detail**

Saelith’s grip begins to fail.

**28 — Close on Saelith**

**Saelith:** Father.

### Movement III — The hand released

**29 — Tall negative-space panel**

Malachar and Saelith appear small at opposite edges of the failing seam.

**30 — Close two-shot**

**Saelith:** Do not turn my choice into permission for the next death.

**31 — Close on Malachar**

**Malachar:** Then what do I do with it?

**32 — Close on their joined hands**

**Saelith:** Carry it.

**33 — Narrow silent gutter**

His fingers loosen by one degree.

**34 — Close on Saelith**

**Saelith:** Do not own it.

**35 — Signature vertical panel**

Their hands separate across a single breaking cyan filament. The city lights continue moving far below.

**36 — Black panel**

**Caption:** He opened his hand.

**37 — Wide panel**

The lower road clears. The final evacuee crosses.

**Caption:** The road held.

**38 — White panel fading to black**

**Caption:** Saelith did not return.

### Movement IV — The wound remembered

**39 — Full-height aftermath panel**

Malachar kneels alone in the shattered chamber. Saelith’s peacock-teal sash thread crosses the floor.

**40 — Reflection detail**

His real hand is open. In the water, the reflected hand has already closed.

**41 — High-angle panel**

The chamber makes him small. A violet-black fracture begins behind his shadow.

**Caption:** For three thousand years, he remembered only the first half.

**42 — Black panel**

**Saelith, memory:** Carry it.

**43 — Black panel with the faint shape of a closing hand**

**Caption:** He forgot the boundary that made the burden love.

**44 — Final panel**

Three thousand years later. The sealed Shadowfen. Malachar’s closed hand opens when a distant archive says Saelith’s name.

**Saelith, missing memory:** Do not own it.

**End card:** *Continue in Episode 01 — The Voice in the Staff*

## Lettering notes

- Saelith’s balloons stay moonbone with a narrow peacock-teal keyline.
- Malachar’s balloons begin moonbone and acquire a fine antique-gold edge only when he uses infinite sight.
- Captions use a restrained serif on flat ink-black; never place generated text inside art.
- Panel 35 receives no sound effect. Silence is the event.
- The final missing line should be visually present to the reader but unavailable to Malachar.

## Quality gate

- Saelith has a tactical objective, saves visible people, and states the ethical boundary herself.
- Malachar’s proposed rescue would work.
- His restraint is an active choice, not inability.
- The hand release is readable without dialogue.
- No beat treats pain as morally purifying.
- The episode ends by altering the meaning of “carry it,” not by announcing a generic threat.
