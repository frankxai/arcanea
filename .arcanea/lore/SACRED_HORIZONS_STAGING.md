# Arcanea Sacred Horizons — STAGING

**Status:** STAGING — original fictional worldbuilding, awaiting canon review  
**Date:** 2026-09-03  
**Authority:** subordinate to `.arcanea/lore/CANON_LOCKED.md`  
**Research boundary:** informed by comparative mythology; never evidence that historical traditions were secretly Arcanea.

## Design rule

Arcanea transforms **functions and archetypal structures**, not the identities or authority of real religions.

Use this chain:

`historical/literary source → documented motif → comparative question → original Arcanean culture/place/story`

Never:

`historical source → “actually Arcanea”`

Real names such as Kunlun, Shambhala, Xitian, Meru, Eden, Penglai, Avalon, and Shangri-La belong to their source traditions or literature. They may appear in research notes. Arcanean canon should normally use original names.

## The Five Horizon Structures

### 1. The First Mountain

A primordial mountain-complex older than current nations and Academies. It is not “Arcanea's Kunlun” or “Arcanea's Meru”; it is an original sacred geography built from the broader mountain/axis question: **what place lets a civilization imagine vertical orientation between ordinary life, cosmic order, danger, and transcendence?**

**Worldbuilding direction**
- Mountain geography must be ecologically and politically inhabited, not an empty “mystic peak.”
- Different cultures disagree about what the summit means.
- Ancient observatories, seed vaults, abandoned ascent routes, weather systems, pilgrimage infrastructure, and contested archives make it lived-in.
- The summit should not simply grant enlightenment. It exposes the climber to a truth or responsibility they cannot unlearn.

**Story engine:** a protagonist climbs seeking power and discovers that the mountain's most consequential secret is a stewardship obligation.

### 2. The Far-West Road

An old pilgrimage and knowledge-retrieval network extending beyond the political center of Arcanea.

It is built around the question: **what happens when wisdom can only be obtained by leaving the institutions that already claim to possess it?**

**Worldbuilding direction**
- multiple roads and schools rather than one authorized route;
- translation houses and caravan libraries;
- dangerous border crossings and conflicting cosmologies;
- companions whose disagreements matter as much as monsters;
- the journey ends with a return problem: knowledge retrieved is useless until translated into a form home communities can actually inherit.

**Story engine:** a crew is sent west to retrieve a lost text, only to learn that its meaning changes depending on who translates it.

### 3. The Veiled Archive

A hidden civilization or distributed refuge created to preserve knowledge through civilizational collapse.

The core question is: **when does preservation become wisdom, and when does it become withdrawal?**

**Worldbuilding direction**
- not a perfect utopia;
- archives, living crafts, seeds, languages, machines, songs, and witnesses preserved across generations;
- entry requires proof of responsibility, not bloodline purity;
- internal faction: remain hidden versus return and help the wider world;
- preservation has costs: knowledge can calcify when protected from reality for too long.

**Story engine:** the refuge has survived precisely because it never intervened. A new catastrophe makes neutrality morally impossible.

### 4. The Garden Beyond Winter

A sanctuary built around biodiversity, memory, cultivation, healing, and abundance after an age of destruction.

The governing question is: **what does abundance demand from those who inherit it?**

**Worldbuilding direction**
- colossal flora, climate engineering, seed memory, medicinal ecosystems, sacred horticulture;
- beauty is inseparable from maintenance;
- there is a boundary or rule that forces characters to confront possession versus stewardship;
- the garden should be vulnerable to human decisions rather than magically self-maintaining forever.

**Story engine:** opening the Garden could save millions, but uncontrolled extraction would destroy the system that makes salvation possible.

### 5. The Last Horizon

The point where Arcanea's old sacred geography becomes astronomical.

The governing question is: **what becomes sacred when a civilization can finally leave the world that formed it?**

**Worldbuilding direction**
- observatories and star-paths connect older pilgrimage traditions with future exploration;
- interstellar travel does not prove ancient prophecies correct;
- stars remain physical destinations and cultural symbols simultaneously;
- characters face deep-time responsibility: what stories, species, memories, mistakes, and institutions deserve to travel with them?

**Story engine:** the first true voyage beyond Arcanea becomes a debate over what civilization has the right to export.

## Supporting archetypal roles

These are story functions, not priestly offices:

- **The Climber** — gains perspective and returns carrying responsibility rather than superiority.
- **The Navigator** — crosses unmapped space and learns that orientation is ethical as well as geographic.
- **The Archivist** — preserves memory but must decide when preservation requires intervention.
- **The Gardener** — turns abundance into stewardship.
- **The Exile** — understands the value of a home only after losing access to it.
- **The Translator** — moves knowledge between worlds without pretending meanings are identical.
- **The Star-Bearer** — sends something valuable toward recipients they may never meet.

## Visual grammar

Avoid generic “AI fantasy temple” imagery.

Each Horizon needs:
- distinct climate and material culture;
- everyday inhabitants;
- infrastructure that explains how people actually live there;
- one unforgettable silhouette at distance;
- one human-scale ritual or craft;
- one ecological system;
- one unresolved political conflict;
- one rule that creates story pressure.

## Canonization test

A Sacred Horizon concept may move from STAGING only when it:

1. is recognizably Arcanean without borrowing a real sacred name as a shortcut;
2. creates stories rather than lore exposition alone;
3. has material ecology, economics, culture, and conflict;
4. connects to existing Arcanea cosmology without spawning a parallel magic system;
5. preserves the fiction/religion boundary;
6. survives a provenance review against the real sources that inspired the motif.
