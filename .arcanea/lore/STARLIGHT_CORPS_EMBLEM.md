## DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

The Starlight Corps emblem is **not decorative**. It is a **visual oath** — sacred geometry that encodes the founding principles, carries resonance from the Founding Stone, and serves as both identification and protection. Every point of the star corresponds to a principle in the Corps Charter. The emblem glows in darkness because the oath is luminous.

**What the emblem IS:**
- A multi-pointed star whose complexity increases with rank
- A living sigil — infused with starlight during the oath ceremony
- A ward against Shadow corruption (starlight repels Void contamination)
- The first thing hostile forces see before a Corps intervention
- The last thing lost civilians see before rescue

**What the emblem is NOT:**
- A military badge (the Corps is not an army)
- A House crest (Corps members surrender House loyalty)
- A decorative flourish (every line carries meaning)
- Static imagery (the sigil subtly pulses with the wearer's heartbeat)

### The Central Void

At the heart of every star-sigil is a **void circle** — black, perfect, untouched. This is not Shadow. This is Nero's gift: the space where potential lives, the canvas before creation, the silence before the oath.

**Symbolic Load:**
- Nero's aspect (potential, not evil)
- The moment before you chose to stand
- The world you protect (held in the star's embrace)
- The unknown you walk toward

The void circle does NOT glow. It absorbs light. This contrast — the luminous star cradling primordial darkness — is the visual thesis of the Corps: **light that does not fear the dark, because darkness is where the work begins**.

## PART II: RANK PROGRESSION — The Star Grows With You

### Aspirant (3-Pointed Star)

```
        ★
       ╱ ╲
      ╱   ╲
     ★─────★
```

**Meaning:** Foundation. The first oath. The three founding principles (Oath, Admission, Neutrality).

**Visual Character:**
- Simplest form
- Each point is broad, unrefined — the star is learning its shape
- Faint glow (the oath is new, the starlight is still settling)
- Often worn on a leather cord, not yet armor-mounted

**Material:** Burnished steel with silver inlay. The void circle is etched, not set.

**Typical Placement:** Chest center, over the heart.

**Meaning:** Supreme command. The twelve points encode the entire Corps legacy: six Charter principles, three founding Stars (Vaelen, Durenna, Solanthus), the Fallen (remembrance point), the Oath itself, and the Eternal Witness (the stars that saw it all begin).

**Visual Character:**
- **The most complex form**. Approaches the aesthetic of a Guardian's Gate-sigil.
- **Blazing luminescence** — a Stellarch's emblem can be seen from miles away at night, a beacon and a warning
- Each point is a masterwork engraving: scenes from Corps history rendered in microscopic detail
- The void circle contains a **living fragment of the Founding Stone** — the stone Vaelen Ashward carved the first Oath into, cracked by Solanthus' light. This fragment hums faintly at 432 Hz (the World Song frequency — STAGING reference).
- The emblem is **sentient-adjacent** — it knows the names of every Corps member who has ever died, and it whispers them when the Stellarch is alone

**Material:** Unique. Each Stellarch's emblem is forged during their ascension ceremony using metal donated by the six Sectors, starlight from the Founding Night's sky (preserved in stasis), and a single drop of the Stellarch's blood. The process takes three days and must be performed under an open sky. No two Stellarch emblems are identical.

**Typical Placement:** Chest center, heart-level. Mounted on a breastplate that bears the names of all previous Stellarchs in Arcanean runes along the edges.

**Special Property:** The Stellarch emblem can be **seen by the dying**. In the moments before death, even those whose eyes have closed report seeing a twelve-pointed star — the Corps' final promise that no one dies alone in the dark country.

## PART III: EMBLEM VARIATIONS — Context-Specific Forms

### Combat Deployment Emblem

- **Simplified geometry** — rank-appropriate star, void circle, no decorative flourishes
- **Material:** Durable alloy (Shael — self-repairing, truth-bonded)
- **Size:** 3 inches, worn on chest or shoulder
- **Light Behavior:** Dims during stealth operations, flares during active engagement
- **Purpose:** Field identification. Enemy recognition. Morale.

### Ceremonial Emblem

- **Full rank-appropriate complexity**
- **Material:** Ceremonial metals + decorative gems (sapphires for Oceanic Sector, rubies for Ember Sector, etc.)
- **Size:** Larger (5-6 inches for officers)
- **Light Behavior:** Steady radiance
- **Purpose:** Formal events, oath ceremonies, diplomatic functions

### Mourning Emblem

- **Rank-appropriate star inverted (points down)**
- **Material:** Blackened silver
- **Light:** Extinguished (the star goes dark to honor the fallen)
- **Purpose:** Worn when a crew member dies. Remains dark until the funeral rites. Afterward, the star is rekindled during a remembrance ceremony.

### Oath Stone Replica

- **Miniature form (1 inch) of the Founding Stone's inscription**
- **Material:** Stone fragment from Aetherhaven's ruins (limited supply, extremely rare)
- **Purpose:** Given to non-Corps allies who have earned the Corps' eternal debt. Not an emblem of membership, but of **kinship**.

## PART IV: EMBLEM LORE — The Living Sigil

### The Founding Stone Connection

Every Corps emblem contains trace starlight resonance from the **Founding Stone** — the rubble-stone Vaelen Ashward carved the first Oath into. When Solanthus swore the Oath, his silver light cracked the stone and infused it with celestial energy. Fragments of that stone were distributed during the Corps' first decade to craft the earliest emblems.

Modern emblems no longer contain physical stone fragments (the supply was exhausted by Year 150), but they are forged in the Founding Stone's presence during the oath ceremony. The stone's resonance imprints on the metal, creating a harmonic link between every emblem ever made.

**Practical Effect:** Two Corps members wearing emblems can sense each other's presence within 100 yards, even in total darkness. The emblems hum faintly when in proximity — a subsonic frequency (174 Hz, Foundation Gate — LOCKED reference) that most beings cannot consciously hear but feel as a sense of *kinship*.

### The Sunset Clause Mechanism

Per the Fifth Charter Principle, any Corps member may leave at any time. When someone invokes the Sunset Clause, their emblem **dims** over the course of one day, the starlight fading as the oath unbinds.

**The emblem does not shatter**. It does not blacken. It simply goes quiet.

Former Corps members often keep their dimmed emblems. Some bury them. Some return them to Starhold, where they are placed in the Hall of Honored Service. A few have been known to rekindle — when a former member returns and re-swears the Oath, the starlight returns, and the emblem lives again.

**The rekindled are rare, but they are legends.**

### Corruption Resistance

The starlight infused in every emblem repels Shadow corruption (Malachar's taint). A Corps member cannot be fully Voidtouched while wearing an active emblem — the starlight cauterizes Shadow contact before it takes root.

**This is not invincibility.** Overwhelming Shadow exposure can extinguish the emblem. When that happens, the star goes dark, the metal goes cold, and the wearer is alone. It is the Corps' most feared failure state: the moment the light you carry dies.

Every Corps member is trained to **cast off a failing emblem** before it fully darkens, so the corruption cannot use the emblem's connection to spread through the Founding Stone network to other members. Letting your emblem die to protect the Corps is considered the highest sacrifice. Those who do are remembered as **Star-Martyrs**.

- Idle state: Gentle pulse (2-second cycle)

## PART VI: FACTION RELATIONSHIPS — How Others See the Star

### Academy Reactions (by House)

| House | Reaction to Corps Emblem |
|-------|------------------------|
| **Lumina** | Respect. The star is proof of dedication to the light, even outside House structure. |
| **Nero** | Curiosity. The void circle at the star's heart intrigues them — they see kinship in embracing darkness. |
| **Pyros** | Competitive respect. They admire the Corps' combat prowess but resent the Corps' political neutrality. |
| **Aqualis** | Warmth. Many Aqualis mages serve in the Corps. The star is seen as an extension of House values. |
| **Terra** | Solid trust. The Corps' endurance over eight centuries earns Terra's deepest respect. |
| **Ventus** | Mixed. Ventus values freedom; some see the Oath as binding, others see it as the ultimate free choice. |
| **Synthesis** | Admiration. The Corps' universal admission policy aligns with Synthesis philosophy. |

### Enemy Recognition

- **Voidtouched remnants**: Fear. The star repels Shadow. They avoid direct confrontation with active emblems.
- **Void Ascendants** (Malachar's faction, STAGING): Hatred. The Corps is the primary obstacle to their ideology. Ascendant doctrine teaches that the star-sigil is a chain, and freeing Corps members from their "slavery" is a mercy.
- **Gate-Touched Underground** (STAGING): Ambivalence. The Corps accepts Gate-Touched members, but many Underground activists see the Corps as an extension of Academy authority.
- **Common bandits**: Immediate retreat. The star means you are overmatched.

### Civilian Perception

To most civilians, the star-sigil means one thing: **help has arrived**.

In villages near the Shadowfen, children are taught to look for the star. In the Ember Wastes, traders paint star-sigils on their wagons as a prayer for safe passage. In the Oceanic Sector, fishing boats fly star-emblems as courtesy flags when Corps ships pass.

The emblem is not feared. It is **relief made visible**.
