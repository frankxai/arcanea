## Structural notes

**Speech-free budget:** beats **1** (22s), **2** (18s), **5** (16s), **7** (16s),
**10** (0s), **13** (18s), **14** (12s) = **102s of 250s picture — 40.8%**,
against a 20% floor. Measured at stage 8, not estimated.

Seven beats, and they are exactly the complement of the seven that carry
dialogue (3, 4, 6, 8, 9, 11, 12) — **derive the set that way, never by hand.**
**Beat 10 is the turn: an instant, not a span**, so it contributes 0s while
still belonging to the set.

The denominator is **picture (250s), not total (254s)**, and the four-second
card is excluded from numerator and denominator alike. Excluding it from one
and not the other is the picture/total conflation this document warns about two
sections down.

Beat 2 counts — the distant bell is sound, not dialogue — and so does beat 1,
which means **the film's first spoken word arrives at 0:40**. Forty seconds of a
woman building a wall before anyone says anything is the opening argument: she
is competent, she is alone, and the film is in no hurry.

**Why the turn is a rack focus, not a camera move.** The film's grammar has been
static and low for 167 seconds. A dolly or crane on the biggest moment would
break the rule early and spend the ending. Changing *focus* rather than
*position* keeps the rule intact while making the reveal feel like a mind
changing rather than a camera finding. It is also the shot most AI video models
handle worst, which means almost nobody will attempt it — and doing it well is a
craft signature a jury reads instantly.

**Why he sits down.** The film could end at the turn with him certifying her.
That would be a story about an institution correcting itself, which is
bureaucratic and cold. Him sitting down is a story about a person choosing to
take some of the weight — the one action available to him, chosen at cost, in
silence. It converts the film's subject from *recognition* to *company*, which
is the version people will remember.

**Why the last shot has no sound.** Every other entry will land the ending on a
swell. The absence of the bed *is* the emotional event — the audience's body has
been carrying 174 Hz for four minutes without knowing it, and the relief is
physical. Adding music would cover the only truly novel thing in the film.

## The three deliberate imperfections (Doctrine Law 5)

| Shot | What goes wrong |
|---|---|
| Beat 2 | Rain hits the lens and stays for six seconds. Nobody wipes it. |
| Beat 7 | Focus misses on the shaking hands and hunts back — one full breath out of focus. |
| Beat 12 | A gust takes dust across frame and obscures both faces for 1.5 seconds at the emotional peak of the beat. We do not cut around it. |
