## Logline

An Academy apprentice who has never opened a single Gate is sent to the stone
yards to be quietly written off — until the instructor measuring her failure
discovers she is not failing to open a Gate. She is holding one shut.

## The turn

> **They think she has no magic. Actually she has spent all of it, every second
> since she was six, keeping something closed.**

The reversal has to recontextualize, not add. Everything the audience read as
weakness in the first two and a half minutes — the shaking hands, the exhaustion,
the flat affect, the way she can't sleep, the way she leans on walls — was the
cost of the work, visible the whole time, misread by everyone including her.

Turn lands at **2:47**.

## The last shot

She lets go. The pressure that has been in the film since frame one — a 174 Hz
sub nobody consciously heard — **stops.** In the silence, for the first time,
the camera rises. It clears the yard wall and keeps going, and we see that the
door she has been holding is one of hundreds, set into the ground across the
whole valley, and some of them are open.

Cut to black on silence. No music. One title card.

## What this is *not* about

Not about the war. Not about Malachar. Not about the Ten. Not about how magic
works. Not a chosen-one story — she is not special, she is *occupied*, and the
distinction is the whole film.

This field exists to stop forty shots of a mythology that has no business in a
four-minute pilot. Canon is the texture of the background here, never the subject.

## Formal rule

**Camera:** The camera never rises above standing eye-height. Every shot is at
or below the height of a person on their feet, and the ground is in frame or
implied in all of them. The rule breaks exactly once — the final shot — and that
break is the release the film has been withholding.

**Color:** Two families only, both desaturated: Academy warm gold (interiors,
authority, the life she is being excluded from) and yard grey-green (wet stone,
weather, the work). The **forbidden color** is a saturated amber-white — the
light in the seam under the door. It is not in the palette anywhere else and it
appears for a cumulative **4 seconds**.

**Sound:** A 174 Hz sine bed runs continuously from frame one at a level most
viewers feel rather than hear. It never rises, never swells, never resolves.
It stops at 3:58 and does not return.

## Why this premise and not another

The obvious Arcanea pilot is a student arriving at the Academy and discovering
magic. Every entrant with a fantasy world will submit that, because it is what
the world suggests and what the models render most happily. It is the statistical
center of the genre and it is unwinnable.

This premise inverts three things at once:

1. **The magic system is measured wrong by its own institution.** The Academy
   ranks by Gates opened (Apprentice 0–2 → Luminor 9–10). A student at 0 is at
   the bottom of a ladder that cannot see her. That is a real critique of a real
   institution, and it is legible to anyone who has ever been measured by the
   wrong number.
2. **The protagonist's arc is not acquisition, it's recognition.** She does not
   get stronger. Someone finally *sees* the load. Cheaper to shoot and far
   harder to forget.
3. **The genre engine is horror, not fantasy.** Something is pushing on a door.
   Fantasy production design plus horror grammar is a combination the field is
   not making, because the models steer toward awe and away from dread.

It is also, structurally, a *pilot* — it opens a series rather than closing a
short. What is behind the door, why it chose her, and why some of the doors in
the last shot are already open are three seasons of question, planted in four
minutes and answered in none of them.

**Canon flag for the keeper:** `arcanea/.claude/CLAUDE.md` **contradicts
itself** on Foundation's frequency. Its canonical Ten Gates table (L45) has
Foundation at 174 Hz, correctly. Two derived surfaces — the Guardian prose list
(L261) and the Guardian Routing table (L313) — have it at 396 Hz, which is
Fire's.

That is not an isolated typo. The routing table's whole frequency column is
**offset by two rows**: every guardian from Lyssandria to Elara carries the
frequency of the gate two positions later, and Ino and Shinkami are right only
because the offset ran off the end and the last two values got repeated. Eight
of ten are wrong. The prose list at L261 carries the identical offset, so both
derived surfaces almost certainly came from one bad paste that dropped 174 and
285 off the top of the column.

`CANON_LOCKED.md` L55 and the 2026-02-21 locked entry are authoritative and this
film uses **174 Hz**. Tracked in issue #92 — flagged, not fixed here, because
correcting a canon file is the keeper's call and not a film's.

**Canon question for the keeper:** does a mortal holding a Gate closed
contradict anything locked about how Gates open? The film's position is that
Gates are *pressure differentials* and a body can stand in one — but that is a
canon extension and needs a ruling before the script locks. If it is refused,
the same story survives with her holding a *seal* rather than the Gate itself.

## Series promise

Episode 1 asks: what is on the other side, and why her?
The last shot answers neither and asks a third: *who else is holding one, and
who stopped?*

Season shape (one line, not to be developed here): the Academy's ranking system
exists because the institution does not want to count the people holding doors.

## Track fit — Higgsfield Global Film Festival, series-episode

The series-episode track rewards a film that reads as **episode one of something**
rather than a self-contained short. Deliberate choices serving that:

- The turn opens a question instead of resolving one.
- The world is under-explained on purpose — a pilot earns the right to withhold.
- The last shot widens the scope from one person to a system.
- Two named characters, one location, one prop. A pilot proves a *voice*, not a
